## Weather
- Missouri ranked #2 in the U.S. for State Farm hail claims in 2025 (behind Texas); #3 in 2024 (~$580M). https://newsroom.statefarm.com/state-farm-paid-over-56-billion-in-hail-claims-in-2025/
- NWS St. Louis: thunderstorms on 40–50 days/yr in the St. Louis area. https://www.weather.gov/lsx/cli_of_stl
- Mar 14, 2024: NWS — supercells over St. Charles County produced 3 to 3.75 in. hail; 3 in. hail photographed in O'Fallon; ~300 reports that day. https://www.weather.gov/lsx/3_14_2024 . (Aggregator: St. Peters & St. Charles 1.75 in. [UNVERIFIED])
- May 26, 2024: NWS — largest hail 2.75 in. near Saint Charles; 2.0–2.5 in. elsewhere in the county; spotter-estimated 70 mph winds in Saint Peters. https://weather.gov/lsx/5_26_2024
- Nov 4–5, 2024: NWS-confirmed EF-0, 6-mile track in northwestern St. Charles County; minor roof damage to homes. 
- May 16, 2025: EF3 St. Louis tornado (152 mph peak, 12.29-mi path, up to 1.8 mi wide); ~10,000 claims. Hit St. Louis City, not St. Charles County — mention only as regional context. https://www.weather.gov/lsx/05_16_2025
- Apr 28, 2012: widespread golf-ball to baseball hail across St. Charles County (NWS event PDF).
- Climate zone: IECC 4A (mixed-humid).
- St. Charles County Table R301.2 (2021 residential code amendments): weathering "Severe"; frost line 30 in.; design wind 107 mph; ground snow 20 psf; termite moderate-heavy; **Ice barrier underlayment required: NO**. https://www.sccmo.org/DocumentCenter/View/22409/2021-Residential-Building-Code-Amendments
- January ~23°F lows / 40°F highs (freeze-thaw); 35–40 days/yr ≥90°F; muggy season late May–late Sept; May wettest month (~4 in.).
- Algae streaks: Gloeocapsa magma (ARMA) — airborne, feeds on limestone filler, mostly north slopes, mostly cosmetic. Copper-granule algae-resistant shingles exist.

## Code
- Unincorporated county: 2021 St. Charles County Residential Code (based on 2021 IRC). City of St. Charles: 2021 IRC/IBC, 2015 IECC. https://www.stcharlescitymo.gov/1228/Adopted-Building-Codes
- IRC R908.3.1.1: no recover over two or more existing layers → third layer = full tear-off. Also no recover over water-soaked/deteriorated roofs.
- R905.1.2 ice barrier only where Table R301.2 says so → county says NO. Best practice still: ice & water shield at eaves, valleys, penetrations, low-slope sections.
- County amendment R905.2.8.5: drip edge at eaves AND rakes, 2 in. laps, fastened ≤12 in. o.c.; underlayment over drip edge at eaves, under at rakes (exception: metal-wrapped fascia ext. 1 in. under roof covering).
- County amendment R905.2.8.2 valleys: open metal (≥24 in.), two-ply mineral roll, closed-valley smooth roll (≥36 in.), or ASTM D1970 self-adhered.
- County amendments exempt roofing replacement from smoke/CO-alarm upgrade triggers.
- Asphalt shingles need ≥2:12 slope; double underlayment 2:12 to <4:12 (standard IRC).

## Missouri law & insurance
- No statewide roofing contractor license in Missouri; business registration with Secretary of State; some cities license locally. → Site must never say "licensed".
- RSMo 407.725 (verified, https://revisor.mo.gov/main/OneSection.aspx?section=407.725): applies to contractors repairing/replacing roof systems on residential or commercial property. (1) Contractor may not advertise or promise to pay or rebate any part of an insurance deductible (no allowances, discounts, gifts, coupons, credits as inducement). (2) Insured may cancel a contract to be paid from a property/casualty policy before midnight of the 5th business day after receiving written notice from the insurer that all or part of the claim isn't covered. (3) Contractor must give a boldface cancellation-right statement + detachable "Notice of Cancellation" form before signing. (4) Refund all payments within 10 days of cancellation (except emergency services acknowledged in writing).
- HB 3328 (2026) — status unconfirmed → DO NOT MENTION.
- Missouri DCI "Raising the Roof on Insurance Coverage" tool compares roof coverage of top 20 MO insurers; DCI warns on ACV a 20-year-old roof may pay as little as 20% of cost. https://insurance.mo.gov/raising-roof-insurance-coverage
- Many carriers use 1%–2% of Coverage A wind/hail deductibles; DCI example: 2% on $100,000 home = $2,000. Say "many policies", "check your declarations page".
- Some insurers offer discounts for UL 2218 Class 3/4 roofs — "some insurers", never a %.

## Materials
- Lifespans (InterNACHI estimates): architectural ~30 yrs; 3-tab ~20; metal 40–80.
- ASTM D7158 wind classes: D 90 mph, G 120, H 150; ASTM D3161 Class F is the other wind label.
- UL 2218 Class 4 = 2-in. steel ball dropped 20 ft, no crack. IBHS 2025: tested 24 IR shingle products (~95% of market), most "Good", none "Excellent" → "Class 4 isn't hail-proof".
- Fiberglass mat (ASTM D3462) overtook organic felt by ~1982; last organic maker stopped 2011 → essentially every asphalt shingle sold today is fiberglass-mat. Fiberglass page = explainer (3-tab vs architectural, both fiberglass).
- 3-tab still made (not discontinued).
- Stone-coated steel: steel panels + stone chips bonded by acrylic, pressed into shingle/shake/tile profiles; DECRA warranty: lifetime limited non-prorated, wind up to 120 mph, hail up to 2.5 in., transferable within 20 yrs (https://www.decra.com/warranty) — attribute to the manufacturer. Fixr (national, 2021): $7–$15/sq ft installed, under 2 lb/sq ft vs 8–12 lb tile. Brands: DECRA, Unified Steel, Tilcor. Honest cons: cost, noise myths (with solid decking it's not loud), dent possible from extreme hail, granule shedding over time [keep generic], fewer installers.
- Standing seam: concealed fasteners/clips allow thermal movement. Exposed-fastener: cheaper, screws/washers need periodic inspection & replacement (no interval number).
