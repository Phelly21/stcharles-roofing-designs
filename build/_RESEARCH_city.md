## Historic districts
- Review body: Historic Landmarks Preservation and Architectural Review Board ("Landmarks Board"), meets monthly at City Hall. Preservation Planner contact via city Historic Preservation page https://www.stcharlescitymo.gov/1191/Historic-Preservation (don't publish staff names; phone 636-949-3228 is the preservation line).
- SIX local districts + 26 individual landmarks, 3,000+ properties: South Main Preservation District, Historic Downtown District, Frenchtown Preservation District, Commons Preservation District, Landmarks Preservation District, Extended Historic Preservation District.
- National Register (NRHP) districts in the city: St. Charles Historic District (1970; increases 1987/1991/1996), Frenchtown HD (1991), Midtown Neighborhood HD (2014), Lindenwood Neighborhood HD (2016), Commons Neighborhood HD (2017). NRHP listing alone generally does not add local review for a private reroof [phrase: "generally"; advise checking].
- City Code §400.1270(A): Certificate of Appropriateness required before (a) any exterior alteration requiring a building/demolition permit, or (b) any exterior alteration not requiring a permit but affecting a significant exterior architectural feature or exterior appearance. Board reviews within 30 days, guided by Secretary of the Interior's Standards.
- So: material change (asphalt→metal, removing slate, etc.) = COA. Like-for-like = guidelines say "Maintenance of a property does not need approval from the HLPARB unless it will change the exterior appearance" (Frenchtown, South Main); Commons: "Normal maintenance does not require a Certificate of Appropriateness." New color/profile = gray area → call the preservation planner first.
- Real example: June 18, 2018 Landmarks Board agenda — request to "install a new metal roof on the house" at 407 N. Seventh St. (Extended district); item had been tabled in May.

## Design guidelines (scanned 1997–99 PDFs, OCR'd — paraphrase, don't quote as exact)
- Frenchtown (1999): preserve original roof forms; primary-façade roof lines not altered; retain mansard color patterns; retain chimneys; flashing maintained; preserve original roof materials when feasible; allowed replacement materials: documented original OR asphalt, fiberglass or wood shingles, slate, synthetic slate. NO roll roofing visible from the street. METAL IS NOT ON THE LIST. Roll roofing and split shake inappropriate; sawn wood shingles OK where original on Victorians. No skylights on front slopes without precedent; bubble/domed skylights prohibited. Solar collectors not visible from street (special consideration possible). Typical historic colors gray, black, brown.
- South Main (1997): preserve roof forms/angle/orientation, brackets, cornices, parapets, bargeboards; don't remove historic roofing in good condition; replace with similar materials; "low profile asphalt shingles … are appropriate replacements for wood shingles"; historic colors gray/black/brown; tile or slate replaced with matching material whenever feasible; style-appropriate gutters & downspouts required.
- Historic Downtown: same approach; replicate color, shadow lines, texture and pattern; tile/slate in kind where feasible; most downtown commercial buildings have flat roofs (historically tarred/roll roofing).
- Commons (1998): COA before any change in roof line, mansard, parapet, or dormers; normal maintenance no COA; shingles may be asphalt, wood, slate, synthetic slate, fiberglass, or STANDING SEAM METAL; clay tile appropriate on Spanish Revival / some Prairie & Bungalow; roll roofing only on roofs ≤2:12; exposed felt unacceptable; keep original mansard cladding & colored-slate patterns; no bubble skylights visible from street; dormers may not exceed roof peak.
- Extended: general appearance criteria; rooftop mechanical equipment screened from public view; harmonious colors.

## 1. Frenchtown  (page slug: roofing-frenchtown-st-charles)
- Local district ~38 blocks / ~350 properties: roughly N. Fifth/Fourth/Third Sts. (west), Wood/French/Tecumseh (north), alley between N. Second and the Katy Trail (east), Clark St. (south). NRHP district: roughly N. Fifth, Clark and French Sts. and the Missouri River; 102.6 acres; 205 contributing buildings; developed ~1830–1940.
- N. Second Street = historic commercial spine (shops with living quarters above). St. Charles Car Company (1872/73), later American Car & Foundry, between Second St. and the river — by 1910 at least one member of every Frenchtown household worked there (historical marker). Katy Trail along the east edge. Sacred Heart Academy complex (mansard after 1875). 
- Housing: French Colonial/Creole-derived and German vernacular BRICK, some weatherboard, limestone foundations; 1880s–90s cottages 4–6 bays, many built as two-family homes with high basements and galleries; Gothic Revival, Italianate, Second Empire/mansard, 1890s Queen Anne; 1½-story frame houses with dormered side-gabled roofs. Federal-vernacular side-gabled roofs with PARAPET walls and joined chimneys; Creole cottages with porch under extension of main roof.
- Roofer angles: parapet end walls (coping, counterflashing into brick), gallery/porch roofs tied into main roof, dormered mansards (slate patterns), low-slope commercial roofs on N. Second, brick chimneys that must stay (flashing).
- Tract median year built 1938; about half of homes pre-1940.
- School district: St. Charles R-VI (generally).
- Flood: Frenchtown levee protects about 400 homes; FEMA redrew maps in 2016 putting 300+ residents newly in the floodplain (STLPR 2020). Floodplain lots may need a Floodplain Development Permit. Levee-raise status [UNVERIFIED].

## 2. Historic Main Street / Downtown  (slug: roofing-historic-main-street-st-charles)
- Historic Downtown District: ~15 blocks / ~85 properties: Third St. (west), Missouri River (east), Clark St. (north), Madison St. (south).
- South Main Preservation District: ~9 blocks / ~105 properties: alley between Second St. and South Main (west), river (east), Madison St. (north), mostly Boonslick Road (south). Period of significance 1790–1920.
- NRHP St. Charles Historic District (1970): First Missouri State Capitol; Lewis & Clark embarkation point; Greek Revival, Italianate, Late Victorian.
- Downtown commercial buildings: mostly flat/low-slope behind parapets → membrane, parapet coping & flashing, internal drains/scuppers. Residential: brick and frame 1800s homes with steep gables, slate/tile in places.
- Floodplain: river-edge lots — check FIRM per address.

## 3. Midtown & Lindenwood (NRHP neighborhoods west of downtown)  (slug: roofing-midtown-lindenwood-st-charles)
- Midtown Neighborhood HD (NRHP 2014): roughly Clark, Madison, Jefferson, Kingshighway, 2nd and 3rd Sts.; 125.4 acres; 527 contributing buildings; built ~1838–1959. Landmarks: St. Charles County Courthouse, Benton School (1896), St. John's A.M.E. (1872), Immanuel Lutheran (1867). Mostly single-family + many duplexes, 1–2½ stories, brick or frame. Our office (498 S 5th St) is in/next to Midtown [say "a few blocks from"].
- Roof materials recorded in the Midtown nomination: slate (incl. conical tower roofs, patterned slate), standing-seam metal, pressed-metal shingles, asbestos-cement shingles, corrugated metal on outbuildings, terra cotta tile ridge, asphalt. → asbestos-cement shingles need proper handling (abatement rules).
- Lindenwood Neighborhood HD (NRHP 2016): 22 acres, portions of 7 blocks: Gamble St. (west), Elm St. (north), alley between Kingshighway and Houston (east), Watson St. (south); immediately east of Lindenwood University (209 S. Kingshighway); platted ~1900–1905; 108 contributing buildings; Queen Anne, Folk Victorian, Colonial & Dutch Colonial Revival (gambrel), Tudor, Mediterranean, Craftsman/Bungalow. Roofs mostly asphalt, often steeply pitched with wide overhangs, exposed rafter tails; octagonal shingles in gables.
- Both are National Register (not among the six local districts) → generally no local COA for a reroof, but confirm.
- Lindenwood-area tract median 1961 (many 1960s homes around the university). School district St. Charles R-VI.

## 4. New Town at St. Charles  (slug: roofing-new-town-st-charles)
- New Urbanist TND planned by Duany Plater-Zyberk & Co. (2003 charrette); developer Whittaker Homes; groundbreaking Oct. 2003; ~747 acres from six farms; planned ~5,700 units; ~90 acres of lakes and canals (stormwater). Inside City of St. Charles; Hwy 370 access. Top-selling development in a 16-state survey (2005–06).
- Housing types: cottages, rowhouses, carriage houses, single-family, live/work; Craftsman/traditional river-town styles; fiber-cement siding common. Tract median year built 2012; nearly all built 2000+. First 2004–2008 roofs now ~17–22 years old → entering replacement window.
- Roofs: steep traditional gables, porch roofs, rowhouse/party-wall tie-ins (shared roof details between neighbors).
- HOA: New Town at St. Charles General Assembly (NTGA) Architectural Review Form explicitly lists ROOFING (application fee tier with siding/windows/paint); needs plot plan, photos, description, construction detail; ARB review up to 2 weeks; "No work may begin until written approval has been received"; ARB approval doesn't replace city permits. ## 5. Harvester  (slug: roofing-harvester-mo)
- Unincorporated community (named for wheat fields; post office 1881–1901); much annexed by St. Peters. Census "Harvester township" pop. 21,238 (ACS 2020–24). St. Charles mailing addresses 63303/63304. Jurisdiction varies by parcel: some in St. Peters city, some unincorporated St. Charles County → determines permit office & code.
- Roads/landmarks: Old Hwy 94 S, Harvester Rd, MO-94, MO-364 (Page Ave extension); Zion Lutheran Church & School (est. 1884, 3866 S. Old Hwy 94); Harvester Christian Church (off 364 & 94).
- Housing: median year built 1990; 7,617 of 8,505 units single-family detached; 1970s 1,598 / 1980s 1,846 / 1990s 2,782 / 2000s 1,023; pre-1960 only ~240. → 1980s–90s builder subdivisions, many on 1st or 2nd roof; original 3-tab replaced with architectural.
- School district: mostly Francis Howell R-III.
