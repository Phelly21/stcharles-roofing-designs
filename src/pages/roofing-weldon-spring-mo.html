About Weldon Spring

Weldon Spring is a city of about 5,300 people (5,326 in the 2020 census) covering roughly 7.8 square miles in southern St. Charles County. It was platted in 1864 and sits near the Missouri River bluffs, along Highway 94 and close to I-64 and US 40-61. The city's Community Development office is at 5401 Independence Road, and families here are served by the Francis Howell R-III School District, including Independence Elementary and Bryan Middle School.

The city keeps a set of parks and gathering spots, including Liberty Pavilion, Founders Pavilion by the city lake, Freedom Pavilion, Independence Amphitheater, the Red Barn and Huber Park. The small separate village of Weldon Spring Heights, with 93 residents in 2020, sits nearby off Highway 94.

The area around the city carries a lot of history. Near Weldon Spring, the World War II Weldon Spring Ordnance Works was, according to the U.S. Department of Energy, the largest explosives factory in the United States before it closed on August 15, 1945. A uranium processing plant later operated on part of the site from 1955 to 1966. After a cleanup that ran from the late 1980s to 2001, the Department of Energy opened the Weldon Spring Site Interpretive Center, and the 8-mile Hamburg Trail now connects the area to Busch Memorial Conservation Area, Weldon Spring Conservation Area and the Katy Trail.

Housing in Weldon Spring and what's on top of it

Weldon Spring's homes skew larger and more upscale than much of the county. Whitmoor Country Club, with its 36-hole course and its own association, is one of the city's best-known communities, and subdivisions such as Camelot and Enclave add more homes on golf and estate lots. The city's median age of 53.1 is high for the area, and long-life roofing materials come up often in our conversations here.

The brick and stone façade rule

One city ordinance shapes almost every roof here. Weldon Spring requires the front façade of residential buildings to be brick or stone unless the Architectural Review Commission approves otherwise. Homes designed around a masonry front tend to come with steep, articulated rooflines: front-facing gables, hips layered over hips, dormers, turrets, and long runs where the roof dies into a brick or stone wall.

On a brick-front Weldon Spring home, the flashing is the roof's weak point, not the shingles.

Complex roofs, more places to leak

A simple gable roof has two planes and a ridge. A typical large Weldon Spring roof may have a dozen planes, several valleys, a chimney or two, and masonry sidewalls on more than one elevation. Every one of those intersections is a place where installation quality decides whether water stays out.

Roof problems we see in Weldon Spring

  • Masonry wall leaks. Where a roof meets brick or stone, it needs step flashing woven into each shingle course and counterflashing cut into the mortar joint. Surface-mounted metal sealed with caulk eventually lets go.
  • Chimney flashing and crickets. Wide chimneys need a cricket (a small saddle) on the uphill side to split water around them. Missing or undersized crickets show up as stains on the ceiling below.
  • Valley wear. Steep roofs with long valleys carry a lot of water fast. County code allows open metal, roll, or self-adhered valley linings. We recommend self-adhered ice barrier membrane in valleys even though county code doesn't require it.
  • Hot, under-ventilated attics. Large, cut-up roofs often have limited ridge length for exhaust vents, which can cook shingles from below.
  • Hail on high-value roofs. When one storm can mean a large claim, owners start asking about impact-rated materials at replacement time.

Permits and review in Weldon Spring

Weldon Spring contracts with St. Charles County Building and Code Enforcement and uses the county's codes, so building permits here follow the county's rules. The county lists replacing a roof among projects that generally do not require a building permit, and its 2021 residential code calls for drip edge at eaves and rakes and a 107 mph design wind speed. The county does not require ice barrier underlayment, though we still use it in valleys and at walls.

The city also runs its own land-use permit for many projects, and its Architectural Review Commission reviews new home exteriors. Whether a reroof needs either one is something to check with the city's Community Development office before work starts, particularly if you're changing material or color. Whitmoor and other associations may have their own exterior standards as well. Our St. Charles County roof permit guide lays out how Weldon Spring compares with neighboring towns.

Weather and hail in southern St. Charles County

According to the National Weather Service, supercells on March 14, 2024 produced hail of 3 to 3.75 inches over St. Charles County, and on May 26, 2024 hail of 2 to 2.5 inches fell in parts of the county. Missouri ranked second in the nation for State Farm hail claims in 2025, behind only Texas. On a large, steep roof, even a partial hail loss is a big job, which is why we document damage with dated photos you can share with your insurer. Roofing services Weldon Spring homeowners ask for

Replacement on a complex roof

A roof replacement here usually means a full tear-off, new underlayment, all-new step and counterflashing at the masonry, chimney crickets where needed, and a ventilation plan sized for the attic. Most owners choose heavier architectural shingles in a color that works with their brick or stone.

Stone-coated steel for hail and curb appeal

For owners who want a roof built for hail without losing the look of shake or tile, stone-coated steel is a strong option on these homes. DECRA's published warranty, for example, covers hail up to 2.5 inches and wind up to 120 mph. It costs more up front, and we lay out the trade-offs honestly.
