About St. Peters

St. Peters sits directly west of St. Charles along Interstate 70, and with 57,732 residents in the 2020 census it is one of the largest cities in St. Charles County. It incorporated in 1910 and became a city in 1959, but for most of its history it was small: 486 people in 1970. Then it exploded, reaching about 15,700 in 1980 and 40,660 in 1990.

The main corridors are I-70, Mid Rivers Mall Drive, Route 364, Highway 94 and Highway 370. Mid Rivers Mall, the county's largest shopping center, anchors the middle of town. The St. Peters Rec-Plex hosted the 2004 U.S. Olympic Diving Trials, and 370 Lakeside Park covers 500 acres around a 140-acre lake with a 5.5-mile trail. The city maintains 27 parks with more than 1,200 acres and 30-plus miles of trails. Students attend Fort Zumwalt schools, including Fort Zumwalt South and East high schools, or Francis Howell schools such as Francis Howell North.

St. Peters housing and typical roofs

That 1970s-to-1990s boom shaped the housing. Many St. Peters homes were built during those decades: ranches, split-levels, bi-levels and two-story colonials in builder subdivisions, with gable and hip roofs, attached garages and asphalt shingles. Mill Wood dates to about 1976. Spencer Creek, with its West Village, South Village and Nob Hill sections, was built roughly 1977 to 1983.

A house from that era has usually been re-roofed at least once and is often on its second or third roof. Many of the original 3-tab roofs have been replaced with architectural shingles, and some houses still carry two layers from an earlier overlay.

Roof problems we see in St. Peters

Aging decking under older roofs

Forty-plus years of roofs, leaks and attic moisture take a toll on sheathing. On older homes, the decking may be plank boards rather than plywood or OSB, and gaps between boards can be too wide to nail shingles properly. Soft decking near eaves, valleys and chimneys is common. This is the detail that most often affects a St. Peters job, because it is tied to the city's permit trigger.

Two layers and full tear-offs

If your roof already has two layers, the residential code doesn't allow a third, so the next roof has to come down to the deck. That's not a bad thing: tear-off is the only way to find and fix the decking problems above.

Wind damage

Older shingles lose their seal and lift in strong wind, and St. Peters has seen plenty of it. Lifted tabs, creased shingles and missing ridge caps are the typical signs. A roof repair can handle scattered wind damage if the rest of the roof is sound.

Ventilation and attic heat

Some older homes have too little intake ventilation at the soffits, which bakes shingles from below and shortens their life. We check intake and exhaust on every replacement and balance them in the new roof.

St. Peters re-roof permits and code

Unlike the City of St. Charles, St. Peters publishes its own Residential Re-Roofing Requirements. According to those requirements, a permit is needed when:

  • more than 25% of the roof sheathing needs replacing, or
  • the roof covering changes to a different material, such as asphalt shingles to metal.

The requirements also address existing board sheathing whose gaps are too wide. A like-for-like shingle replacement on sound decking generally doesn't need a permit. Because decking condition often isn't known until tear-off, we plan for that possibility in the quote. Confirm your project with the St. Peters Building Department before work starts. Storms in St. Peters

According to the National Weather Service, storms on May 26, 2024 brought spotter-estimated 70 mph winds to St. Peters, and the Weather Service reported hail up to 2.75 inches near Saint Charles during the same outbreak. Two months earlier, on March 14, 2024, supercells produced 3 to 3.75 inch hail across St. Charles County. And on July 26, 2022, St. Peters recorded 12.3 inches of rain, an unofficial record for the area, the kind of downpour that finds every weak flashing joint. Roofing services St. Peters homeowners ask for

  • Full replacement on 1970s–90s homes. A roof replacement here usually means removing one or two layers, replacing damaged sheathing, and installing new underlayment, drip edge, flashing and ventilation to the city's 115 mph standard.
  • Hail-resistant upgrades. Shingles tested to UL 2218 Class 4 resist impact better than standard shingles, though none are hail-proof. Some insurers offer a discount for them.
  • Material changes. Metal roofing and stone-coated steel both last longer than asphalt; under St. Peters' requirements, switching materials calls for a permit.
  • Checkups. A roof inspection after a storm or before a sale shows what shape the roof is in.
