The short answer, and why it isn't the whole answer

St. Charles County is a patchwork. The City of St. Charles, St. Peters and Cottleville each run their own building departments. Weldon Spring contracts with the county. Unincorporated areas like Orchard Farm and parts of Harvester fall under the county directly, and the river towns of Portage des Sioux and West Alton sit almost entirely in regulated floodplain. Which office has authority depends on your parcel, not your mailing address. A St. Charles mailing address in Harvester, for example, may be inside St. Peters city limits or in unincorporated county.

On top of building permits sit three other kinds of approval that catch homeowners off guard: historic district review in the City of St. Charles, floodplain development permits in the river bottoms, and homeowners association architectural review in planned communities.

City of St. Charles: no permit, with two caveats

The City of St. Charles Building Division lists "Replacing roof shingles" under projects that generally do not require permits. The same page adds that permit-exempt projects may still require Landmarks Board approval in the Extended Historic District, and that a floodplain development permit may be required in the floodplain.

The city enforces the 2021 International Residential Code for submittals on or after January 1, 2023. Even without a permit, the work must meet that code: no new layer over two or more existing layers (a third layer means full tear-off), shingles only on slopes of 2:12 or steeper, and double underlayment between 2:12 and 4:12. The city's design table lists a 107 mph ultimate design wind speed and a "Severe" weathering rating, and it does not require ice barrier underlayment, though we still install ice and water shield in valleys and around penetrations as good practice.

If the job goes beyond shingles, such as sistering rafters, adding a dormer or skylight, changing the pitch or replacing large areas of decking, call Community Development at 636-949-3222 before work starts. The city doesn't publish an exact line, so we won't guess one.

Historic districts: the review that matters more than the permit

The city has six local historic districts: South Main, Historic Downtown, Frenchtown, Commons, Landmarks and the Extended Historic Preservation District. Under City Code §400.1270, a Certificate of Appropriateness is required before any exterior alteration that needs a building permit and before any exterior alteration that doesn't need a permit but affects a significant architectural feature or the exterior appearance. Switching from asphalt to metal, or removing slate, falls in that second group. Like-for-like maintenance generally does not.

Homes in Frenchtown and around Historic Main Street are inside local districts. Midtown and Lindenwood are National Register districts, which generally don't add local review for a private reroof, but confirm for your address. Our full guide to replacing a roof in a St. Charles historic district walks through each district's guidelines.

St. Peters: its own re-roofing requirements

St. Peters adopted the 2021 IRC in December 2023 and publishes "Residential Re-Roofing Requirements." Those requirements say a permit is needed when more than 25 percent of the sheathing must be replaced or when the roof covering changes to a different material, such as shingles to metal. Board sheathing with gaps too wide for shingles has also been reported as a trigger. A straight shingle-for-shingle replacement does not need a permit. Confirm with the St. Peters Building Department before work starts on your St. Peters roof.

One code detail stands out: St. Peters' design table uses a 115 mph design wind speed, higher than the county's 107 mph. Drip edge is required.

Cottleville and Weldon Spring

Cottleville issues its own permits under the 2021 IRC and IBC. Roofing is not on the city's list of example projects that require a permit, but that list is not exhaustive, so call Cottleville City Hall to confirm before a reroof.

Weldon Spring contracts with St. Charles County Building and Code Enforcement and uses county codes, so the county rule applies: replacing a roof generally does not require a building permit. The city also has its own land-use permit for many projects. Whether a reroof needs one hasn't been confirmed, so check with the city's Community Development office.

Unincorporated county and the floodplain rule

For unincorporated areas, the county's building permit information lists "Replacing a roof" among projects that generally do not require permits. The county code requires drip edge at both eaves and rakes, with 2-inch laps and fasteners no more than 12 inches apart, and roof replacement does not trigger smoke or CO alarm upgrades.

The floodplain is where people get surprised. The county's floodplain development permit page states that floodplain permits are required even when a building permit is not, and it names reroofing a structure as an example. That covers the floodway, floodway fringe and density floodway on the 2016 flood maps, which takes in much of Orchard Farm and north St. Charles County.

A second rule applies after big losses. If the cost to repair a substantially damaged structure reaches 50 percent of its pre-damage market value, the whole structure must be brought into compliance, including a lowest floor at least one foot above base flood elevation. A roof alone rarely reaches that, but a roof plus flood repairs can.

Portage des Sioux and West Alton

Portage des Sioux has its own floodplain ordinance. Who issues building permits there isn't confirmed, so call the city before any exterior work. West Alton posts a land-use permit form and a floodplain development permit form whose development types include routine maintenance and minor improvement. Contact West Alton City Hall at 111 Richard Dr. before work starts. Many West Alton homes have been raised on piers, which changes staging and access for any roof job.

Don't forget the HOA

Planned communities add their own approval. The New Town at St. Charles General Assembly's architectural review form explicitly lists roofing, asks for a plot plan, photos, a description and construction details, allows up to two weeks for review, and states that no work may begin until written approval is received. That approval doesn't replace any city requirement. In other subdivisions, check your indentures or trustees. We'll provide the product specs and color samples you need for the form.

No state license in Missouri

Missouri has no statewide roofing contractor license, and St. Charles County doesn't license roofers as a trade. That makes the written quote, local references and a clear scope of work your best protection.
