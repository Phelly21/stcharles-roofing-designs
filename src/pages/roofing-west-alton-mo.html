West Alton at the meeting of two rivers

West Alton covers about 37 square miles of river-bottom land at the confluence of the Missouri and Mississippi rivers, directly across the Mississippi from Alton, Illinois. U.S. 67 and Highway 94 are the main roads, the ZIP code is 63386, and students attend the Orchard Farm R-V School District. City Hall is at 111 Richard Dr.

The land is large but the population is small and shrinking. West Alton counted 573 people in 2000, 522 in 2010 and 359 in the 2020 census. Much of that change is the result of repeated flooding and buyouts. The area is also one of the best places in the region to see the rivers up close. Edward "Ted" and Pat Jones–Confluence Point State Park sits at the tip of the peninsula where the two rivers join. The Audubon Center at Riverlands draws birders, and the Missouri Department of Conservation manages the West Alton and Dresser Island areas. In town, West Alton City Park and Memorial Park, with its Veterans Memorial, are community gathering spots, and Rivers Pointe Fire Department covers the area.

Raised homes and what they mean for a roof

In June 2024, West Alton's mayor told the Associated Press that many of the town's remaining homes have been raised and that buyouts had cleared nearly all homes on Ely Street and in areas exposed to Bear Creek. That shapes nearly every roofing job here.

On a raised house, the roof is simply farther from the ground. That affects how we set ladders, where staging goes, how shingles and panels get up to the roof, and how old material comes down without damaging siding, stairs or the yard below. It also affects the roof itself. Homes that were lifted often have new porches, stair covers or entry roofs added at the new height, and the joints between those additions and the main roof are common leak points. We check every tie-in, wall flashing and step flashing run during our visit.

Elsewhere you will still find older homes at grade, metal-roofed garages and outbuildings, and low-slope roofs over porches. Each gets looked at on its own terms.

Roof problems we see in West Alton

  • Wind-lifted shingles and edges. Open bottomland lets wind build speed. Rakes, eaves and ridges take the worst of it, especially on roofs without full drip edge and sealed starter courses.
  • Flashing failures at additions. Raised homes with added porches and stair roofs have more roof-to-wall joints, and each one depends on correct step and counter flashing.
  • Moisture in the decking. Long periods of high water and humidity can leave sheathing soft or stained. We photograph what we find in the attic and on the deck so you can see it for yourself.
  • Worn metal roof fasteners. Exposed-fastener metal on homes and outbuildings uses screws with rubber washers that dry out and loosen over time.
  • Roofs too low for shingles. Asphalt shingles need at least a 2:12 slope, and slopes from 2:12 to under 4:12 need double underlayment. Flatter porch roofs need a different material.

Permits in West Alton: contact City Hall first

Replacing a roof generally does not require a building permit in unincorporated St. Charles County, and many homeowners assume the same is true everywhere. In a floodplain town, that assumption is risky. The City of West Alton posts a Land Use Permit form and a separate Floodplain Development Permit form, and the floodplain form lists development types that include "Routine Maint" and "Min Improvement." St. Charles County's own floodplain program goes further and names reroofing a structure as work that needs a floodplain development permit even when no building permit is required.

Our advice

Contact West Alton City Hall before any exterior work, including a reroof, and ask which permits apply to your property. We set the start date once you have the answer. Our St. Charles County roof permit guide explains how the rules differ between towns.

Flood insurance and the substantial-damage rule

West Alton homeowners often deal with flood coverage and floodplain rules at the same time as roof repairs. Under the substantial-damage rule used in St. Charles County floodplains, if repairs to a substantially damaged structure cost 50% or more of its pre-damage market value, the structure must be brought into compliance, with the lowest floor at least one foot above base flood elevation. A roof can count toward that total when it is part of a larger repair. Get the city's answer on how costs are counted before you combine projects.

Floods, buyouts and storms at the confluence

West Alton has flooded repeatedly, and St. Charles County's buyout notices list the 1973, 1993, 2011 and 2019 floods. During the record 1993 flood, the Mississippi crested at 38.17 feet at the Grafton gauge on August 1, 1993. After the 2019 flood, which put water into more than 900 homes across the county's river areas, the county launched a voluntary buyout program funded with about $15.7 million in federal HUD disaster recovery money. It aims to buy up to about 100 homes, keeps the land as open space, includes West Alton, and is expected to wrap up by early 2027.

Wind and hail add to the picture. According to the National Weather Service, supercells dropped hail of 3 to 3.75 inches over St. Charles County on March 14, 2024, and hail up to 2.75 inches fell near St. Charles on May 26, 2024. The NWS also confirmed an EF-0 tornado on November 4–5, 2024, along a 6-mile track in northwestern St. Charles County, with minor roof damage to homes. Roof replacement on raised homes

When a roof is at the end of its life, a full roof replacement on a raised home includes planned access and staging, tear-off to the deck, replacement of damaged sheathing, drip edge at eaves and rakes as the county code requires, and ice and water shield in valleys and around penetrations. Ice barrier isn't required by code in St. Charles County, but it adds protection against wind-driven rain.

Metal and stone-coated steel

For many West Alton homes, metal roofing is the long-term answer: light weight on an elevated structure, long life and good wind performance. Homeowners who want a shingle or shake look with metal's durability can consider stone-coated steel shingles.
