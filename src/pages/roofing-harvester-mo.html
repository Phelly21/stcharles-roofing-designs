About Harvester

Harvester takes its name from the wheat fields that once covered this part of St. Charles County, and it had its own post office from 1881 to 1901. Today it is an unincorporated community rather than a city, and over the decades much of it has been annexed by St. Peters. The Census Bureau's Harvester township counts about 21,000 residents in recent estimates.

The main roads are Old Highway 94 South, Harvester Road, Missouri 94 and Route 364, the Page Avenue extension that links the area to St. Louis County. Zion Lutheran Church and School, founded in 1884 on South Old Highway 94, is one of the community's oldest institutions, and Harvester Christian Church sits near the 364 and 94 interchange. Most of Harvester is in the Francis Howell R-III school district, and residents use St. Charles mailing addresses in the 63303 and 63304 ZIP codes.

Harvester housing and typical roofs

Harvester is overwhelmingly single-family. Census figures show roughly 7,600 of its 8,500 housing units are detached houses, with a median year built of 1990. The building came in waves: about 1,600 homes from the 1970s, 1,850 from the 1980s, 2,800 from the 1990s and another 1,000 from the 2000s. Only around 240 homes predate 1960.

That means builder subdivisions, mostly ranches, split-levels and two-stories with gable and hip roofs, attached garages and a few valleys where wings meet. Many of these houses were built with 3-tab shingles and are now on their first or second replacement. When they're re-roofed today, almost all go to architectural shingles, which have largely replaced 3-tab as the standard.

Roof problems we see in Harvester

Two layers already on the roof

Laying a new layer of shingles over the old one has long been a common way to save on a re-roof. If a Harvester house has two layers now, the residential code doesn't allow a third, so the next roof is a full tear-off down to the decking. Tear-off also lets us see the decking, which is the only way to know what's under there.

Worn decking and ventilation

Thirty- and forty-year-old roof decks often have soft spots near eaves, around chimneys and under old leaks. Some older homes also have too little attic ventilation, which cooks shingles from below and shortens their life. We check intake and exhaust on every job and balance them in the new roof.

Wind-lifted shingles and failing flashing

Aging 3-tab shingles lose their seal strips, and the first strong wind lifts or tears the tabs. Chimney flashing, sidewall step flashing and pipe boots on homes this age are also frequent leak sources. Many of these can be fixed with a targeted roof repair when the rest of the roof still has life.

Switching materials?

Moving from asphalt shingles to metal or stone-coated steel on a St. Peters parcel counts as a change in roofing material under the city's published requirements, so expect a permit. On a county parcel, confirm with County Building & Code Enforcement.

Storms in the Harvester area

Harvester sits between St. Charles and St. Peters, and the storms that hit those cities hit it too. According to the National Weather Service, supercells on March 14, 2024 dropped hail of 3 to 3.75 inches over St. Charles County. On May 26, 2024 the Weather Service reported hail up to 2.75 inches near Saint Charles and spotter-estimated 70 mph winds in neighboring St. Peters. After a storm, our storm and hail damage service documents what we find with dated photos you can share with your insurer.

Roofing services Harvester homeowners ask for

  • Full tear-off and replacement. For a 1980s or '90s house on its second or third roof, a roof replacement includes removing every layer, replacing bad decking, new underlayment, drip edge at eaves and rakes, and rebuilt flashing and ventilation.
  • Impact-rated shingle upgrades. Some Harvester owners choose architectural shingles tested to UL 2218 Class 4 for hail; some insurers offer a discount for them, so ask yours.
  • Metal and stone-coated steel. For a longer-lasting roof, metal roofing and stone-coated steel are options. Remember the permit question above if you're on a St. Peters parcel.
  • Pre-sale or post-storm checkups. A roof inspection tells you how much life is left and whether repairs will do.

How a Harvester re-roof comes together

  1. Confirm the jurisdiction. We ask for your address and check whether the parcel is in St. Peters or unincorporated county, because that sets the permit rules and the design wind speed.
