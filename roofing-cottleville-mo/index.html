Cottleville at a glance

Cottleville is a small city in central St. Charles County, about 4.7 square miles, with City Hall at 5490 Fifth Street. Its roots go back to 1798, when Capt. Warren G. Cottle settled here on a Spanish land grant; Lorenzo Cottle platted the town and gave it its name in 1839. For most of the next 150 years it stayed tiny. The 1980 census counted 184 residents.

That changed quickly. Cottleville reached 5,611 people in 2020, and its population grew 82.5% between 2010 and 2020 alone. Highway N is the main road into Old Town Cottleville, running east toward Mid Rivers Mall Drive and west toward Highway K, and the city sits close to the Route 364, Highway 94 and Mid Rivers Mall Drive interchange. St. Charles Community College is here, along with Legacy Park, Scott A. Lewis Park, Hansen Park, College Meadows Park, Lorenzo Plaza and the Cottleville Weldon Spring Rotary Amphitheater. Most of the city is in the Francis Howell School District, served by Francis Howell Central High, Saeger Middle and Warren Elementary.

Cottleville's housing and the roofs on it

Because the city grew so late, most Cottleville homes were built after 1980, and a large share went up in the 2010s and 2020s. Cottleville Trails is a good example: a newer HOA community with a pool, lakes and trails, where new construction was still underway in 2023 and 2024. Around it are subdivisions of two-story and ranch homes with attached garages, gable and hip roofs, and the occasional bump-out or covered patio.

That age profile shapes almost every roofing conversation here. Many Cottleville homeowners are dealing with the first roof the house has ever had, and that roof was usually chosen by a builder working to a budget, not by the person who now owns it.

Builder-grade roofs reaching their first replacement

The first wave of subdivision roofs from the late 1990s and 2000s is aging into replacement range. Architectural shingles are commonly estimated to last around 30 years and 3-tab shingles around 20, but a builder-grade roof on a south- or west-facing slope often shows wear sooner: granule loss, brittle tabs and lifted shingles along the rakes. When it's time, a full roof replacement is the chance to fix what the builder skipped.

Young roofs with storm damage

Newer homes do not get a pass from hail. A roof under 15 years old can still need a claim after a single storm. On a young roof the question is usually whether hail bruised the shingle mat or just knocked off granules, and that is what dated photos help you and your adjuster sort out.

Roof problems we see in Cottleville

  • Missing or poorly placed drip edge. Current residential code calls for drip edge at both eaves and rakes. A roof installed with drip edge only at the eaves, or none at all, lets water wick into fascia and decking edges.
  • Thin valley protection. Two-story homes with intersecting rooflines dump a lot of water into valleys. Ice barrier membrane isn't required by county code, but we recommend it in valleys and around penetrations.
  • Pipe boots and box vents. Rubber pipe collars crack in the sun long before the shingles wear out. It's one of the most common small roof repairs on newer homes.
  • Ventilation shortfalls. Attics with too little intake at the soffits run hot in summer, which ages shingles early and can affect coverage under a shingle manufacturer's warranty.
  • Layered roofs in Old Town. Older homes near Highway N sometimes carry two layers of shingles. Code does not allow a recover over two or more layers, so the next roof means a full tear-off and a look at the decking underneath.

Permits and HOA approval in Cottleville

Cottleville runs its own building permit program under the 2021 International Residential and Building Codes, so it does not use St. Charles County's permit office the way some nearby areas do. Roofing is not on the city's published list of example projects that require a permit. That list gives examples rather than a complete rulebook, so call Cottleville City Hall to confirm before work starts, especially if decking, rafters, skylights or the roof's shape will change.

The bigger hurdle for many Cottleville homeowners is the HOA. Subdivision indentures and architectural guidelines often say something about roof color or material, and rules differ from one association to the next. Check with your HOA or trustees before you commit. We'll provide product names, color samples and spec sheets for an approval request. Hail, wind and insurance in Cottleville

Central St. Charles County takes its share of severe weather. According to the National Weather Service, supercells on March 14, 2024 dropped hail of 3 to 3.75 inches over St. Charles County, and the storms of May 26, 2024 brought hail of 2 to 2.5 inches across parts of the county along with spotter-estimated 70 mph winds in neighboring St. Peters. After storms like these, out-of-town crews often go door to door through subdivisions.

On a young roof, the right question isn't "is it old?" but "did that storm break the shingle?"

If you think your roof took a hit, start with our storm and hail damage page. We document what we find with dated photos you can share with your insurer. Before you sign anything, read our guide to your rights on a Missouri hail claim. Under RSMo 407.725, no roofer may offer to cover your deductible, and you can cancel a claim-funded contract within five business days of a written denial.

What Cottleville homeowners ask us for

A first replacement done right

For a 20- to 25-year-old subdivision roof, the usual move is a tear-off to the deck, new underlayment, drip edge at eaves and rakes, ice barrier in the valleys and new flashing at walls and chimneys. Most homeowners choose architectural shingles, which have largely replaced the flat 3-tab look in these neighborhoods.

An upgrade for hail

Owners who have already been through one hail claim often ask about impact-rated shingles or stone-coated steel. Some insurers offer discounts for roofs rated to UL 2218 Class 3 or Class 4, so it's worth asking your carrier before you choose.

A second opinion

If a storm crew told you the whole roof is shot, a roof inspection with photos gives you something to compare. Sometimes the honest answer is a repair.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Cottleville?

Cottleville issues its own building permits under the 2021 IRC and IBC, and roofing does not appear on the city's published list of example projects that require a permit. That list is not exhaustive, so call Cottleville City Hall at 5490 Fifth Street before work starts, especially if decking, rafters or the roof's shape will change. Your subdivision's HOA may also want to approve the shingle color.

My Cottleville home is only 10 to 15 years old. Can the roof really need replacing?

Usually not from age alone. Architectural shingles are commonly estimated to last around 30 years. What ends a young roof early is storm damage, poor attic ventilation or installation shortcuts like missing drip edge or skimpy valley flashing. If shingles are cracked, bruised or blowing off on a newer roof, have it looked at and photographed before deciding between a repair and a replacement.

Should I upgrade from the builder's original shingles when I replace my roof?

Many original Cottleville roofs were standard builder-grade shingles. At the first replacement, homeowners often move to a heavier architectural shingle, an impact-rated shingle tested to UL 2218 Class 4, or stone-coated steel. Some insurers offer discounts for impact-rated roofs, so ask your carrier. We will price the options side by side in a free written quote.

Will my HOA in Cottleville Trails or another subdivision need to approve my new roof?

Many Cottleville subdivisions have homeowners associations, and newer communities like Cottleville Trails are HOA neighborhoods. Roof color and material rules vary by association and are set in each subdivision's indentures or architectural guidelines. Check with your HOA or trustees before you order shingles. We can give you product names, color samples and spec sheets to include with any approval request.

Is a roofer allowed to cover my hail deductible in Missouri?

No. Missouri law, RSMo 407.725, bars roofing contractors from advertising or promising to pay or rebate any part of your insurance deductible, including through discounts, credits or gifts. The same law lets you cancel a claim-funded roofing contract within five business days after your insurer tells you in writing that all or part of the claim is not covered. A roofer offering to eat your deductible is a red flag.

Do you work on the older homes in Old Town Cottleville?

Yes. Old Town Cottleville along Highway N has a mix of older homes and small commercial buildings that look nothing like the newer subdivisions around it. These roofs tend to have more patching, older decking and sometimes more than one layer of shingles. Code does not allow a new roof over two existing layers, so those jobs become full tear-offs.
