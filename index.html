A local roofer

The roofs around our office are the roofs we know best

From 498 S 5th St., we're a few blocks from Historic Main Street and minutes from St. Peters, Cottleville and Weldon Spring.

St. Charles Roofing Designs replaces and repairs roofs for homeowners and businesses across St. Charles and the nearby St. Charles County towns. Most of our work is residential: full roof replacements, leak and flashing repairs, and hail and wind damage after the spring storms. We also handle commercial roofs for offices, shops and multi-family buildings.

St. Charles is an unusual roofing town. Close to the river, half the homes near our office were built before 1940: steep brick houses with parapet walls, dormered mansards and chimneys the city's design guidelines say must stay. A few miles west, St. Peters and Harvester are full of 1970s, '80s and '90s subdivisions on their second or third roof. North of Route 370, New Town's first roofs from 2004 onward are starting to reach the end of their life. Each needs a different conversation, and we have all three every week.

Our approach is simple. We look at the roof before we quote it, we photograph what we find, and we tell you whether a repair will do or it's time for a new roof. When it is, we'll walk you through architectural shingles, metal roofing and stone-coated steel, and what each costs over the life of the roof. Our guide to what a new roof costs in St. Charles is a good place to start.

Field notes

Three things every St. Charles homeowner should know

Local facts that change how you plan a roof, sourced from the National Weather Service, the city and Missouri law.

Hail here is serious

On March 14, 2024 the National Weather Service documented 3 to 3.75-inch hail over St. Charles County. That May it logged 2.75-inch hail near St. Charles. Missouri ranked second in the country for State Farm hail claims in 2025. Read our hail history.

Shingles usually don't need a permit

The City of St. Charles lists replacing roof shingles among projects that generally don't need a building permit. Historic districts and floodplain lots are the exceptions, and St. Peters has its own re-roof rules. See the town-by-town guide.

Missouri protects you after a storm

Under RSMo 407.725, no roofer may offer to pay or rebate your insurance deductible, and you can cancel a claim-funded contract within five business days after a written denial. Know your rights.

Built for our weather

What a roof needs to last in St. Charles County

The city rates our weathering as "severe." We get hard spring hail, 90-degree summers, and winters that freeze and thaw over and over. The details under the shingles decide whether a roof makes it.

  • Drip edge at eaves and rakes. St. Charles County's code amendments spell it out, and it keeps water off the fascia and decking.
  • Ice and water shield where it counts. Local code doesn't require an ice barrier, but we recommend self-adhered membrane in valleys, around chimneys and on low-slope sections.
  • A real tear-off when it's due. Code allows at most two layers. A third means everything comes off, and we inspect the decking underneath.
  • Balanced ventilation. Attic heat cooks shingles from below. Intake at the eaves and exhaust at the ridge keeps the deck cooler and drier.

Thinking about impact-rated shingles? Read Class 4 vs. Class 3 shingles in Missouri first. They help, but they aren't hail-proof.

Historic homes

Roofing in Frenchtown, South Main and the Commons

St. Charles has six local historic districts with more than 3,000 properties, and each has design guidelines. Frenchtown's guidelines allow asphalt, fiberglass or wood shingles, slate and synthetic slate, but they don't list metal and they bar visible roll roofing. The Commons guidelines do allow standing seam metal. Change a roof's material or look in a district, and the city's Landmarks Board may need to sign off first.

We plan for that from the start. See historic home roofing, our Frenchtown page, and the full guide to replacing a roof in a St. Charles historic district.

Questions St. Charles homeowners ask us

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in St. Charles?

In the City of St. Charles, replacing roof shingles is on the city's list of projects that generally don't need a building permit. Two exceptions matter: homes in the city's historic districts may need Landmarks Board approval if the work changes the roof's appearance, and properties in a floodplain may need a floodplain development permit. St. Peters, Cottleville and unincorporated St. Charles County each have their own rules, which we cover in our permit guide.

How much does a new roof cost in St. Charles?

It depends on the size and pitch of the roof, how many layers have to come off, how much decking needs replacing, and the material. National cost guides put an average asphalt roof replacement around $9,500, with a wide range from roughly $5,800 to over $40,000 for large or premium roofs. We give a free written quote after looking at your roof, so you see the real number for your house.

Will my homeowners insurance pay for hail damage?

If hail or wind damaged the roof, a homeowners policy often covers repair or replacement minus your deductible. Many Missouri policies now use a wind and hail deductible of 1% or 2% of the home's dwelling coverage, and some pay older roofs on an actual cash value basis. Check your declarations page. By Missouri law, no roofer may offer to pay or rebate your deductible.

How do I know if my roof needs to be replaced or just repaired?

A roof usually needs replacing when shingles are curling, cracking or losing granules across large areas, when there are leaks in several places, or when it has reached the end of its expected life, about 20 years for 3-tab shingles and about 30 for architectural shingles. Isolated problems like a cracked pipe boot, a few blown-off shingles or a failed flashing can usually be repaired.

Do you work in Frenchtown and the other historic districts?

Yes. Our office is on South Fifth Street, a few blocks from Frenchtown and Historic Main Street, and many of the homes we work on are in the city's historic districts. Changing a roof's material or appearance in those districts can require a Certificate of Appropriateness from the city's Landmarks Board, so we plan the material choice with that review in mind.
