Based in St. Charles

Our office sits on South Fifth Street, a few blocks from Historic Main Street and Frenchtown. That matters more than it sounds. The roofs we work on most are the ones around us: brick and frame homes in the city's historic districts, ranch and split-level subdivisions from the 1960s through the '80s, and newer two-story homes in St. Peters, Cottleville and Weldon Spring. Each brings its own problems, from steep, cut-up roofs with chimneys and dormers to low-slope porch roofs that were never meant to hold shingles.

Being local also means we know the rules. Re-roofing with shingles doesn't need a permit in the City of St. Charles, but homes in the city's historic districts go through a separate design review. Other towns and unincorporated St. Charles County have their own requirements. We explain those in our roof permit guide for St. Charles County.

How we work

Every job, big or small, follows the same pattern.

  1. We look before we quote. We get on the roof (and in the attic where we can) and photograph what we find, so you're not taking our word for it.
  2. We tell you what it actually needs. If a repair will buy you years, we'll say so. If the roof is at the end of its life, we'll explain why and what a replacement involves.
  3. We build to the code and the manufacturer's instructions. That means the right underlayment, drip edge at eaves and rakes, proper valley and wall flashing, and balanced ventilation. These are the details that decide whether a roof reaches its rated life.
  4. We clean up and walk you through it. Nails, scrap and old shingles leave with us, and we go over the finished roof with you.

What we install

Most St. Charles homes get architectural shingles, the dimensional asphalt shingle that has largely replaced the old flat 3-tab look. We also install traditional fiberglass 3-tab shingles, metal roofing, and stone-coated steel for homeowners who want a roof built for hail. For businesses we handle commercial roofing, and for older homes in the historic districts we offer historic home roofing.

Straight answers on storms and insurance

Hail is a fact of life here. The National Weather Service documented hail up to 3.75 inches over St. Charles County on March 14, 2024, and 2.75-inch hail near St. Charles that May. After storms like that, out-of-town crews knock on doors. We'd rather you know your rights first. Missouri law (RSMo 407.725) bars any roofer from offering to pay or rebate your insurance deductible, and it lets you cancel a claim-funded roofing contract within five business days after your insurer denies the claim in writing. Our guide to Missouri hail claims covers the details, and our storm and hail damage page explains how we document damage.
