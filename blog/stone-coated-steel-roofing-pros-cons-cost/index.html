Stone-Coated Steel Roofing in St. Charles: Pros, Cons and Cost

An honest look at stone-coated steel for St. Charles County homes: how it is made, what it does well in hail, where it falls short, and what national cost guides say.

By St. Charles Roofing Designs · October 18, 2026

Get a Free Quote(636) 242-5111

Stone-coated steel roofing is steel panels coated with stone chips and pressed into shingle, shake or tile shapes. For St. Charles homeowners, its main strengths are hail resistance, light weight and long manufacturer warranties; its downsides are a higher upfront cost and fewer installers. St. Charles Roofing Designs installs it and gives free written quotes.

What stone-coated steel actually is

A stone-coated steel panel starts as a sheet of steel. Stone chips are fixed to the surface with an acrylic coating, and the panel is pressed into a profile that mimics a traditional roof: dimensional shingles, wood shake, or clay tile. From the street, a well-installed stone-coated roof reads as a textured shingle or tile roof, not as metal.

Several manufacturers make it, including DECRA, Unified Steel and Tilcor. Each sells its own product lines, profiles, accessories and warranty terms, so the details below are general unless we name the manufacturer.

Common profiles

  • Shingle profile: the closest match to the architectural shingles on most St. Charles County homes. A good choice when you want the neighborhood look with a steel roof underneath.
  • Shake profile: deep, rough-cut texture meant to look like wood shake, without the rot, splitting or fire concerns of real wood.
  • Tile profile: barrel or flat tile shapes for Mediterranean and Spanish-style homes, at a fraction of real tile's weight.

The pros

Strong hail performance

This is the reason most people in St. Charles County ask about it. Steel does not crack the way an asphalt shingle can, and the stone coating helps hide minor surface marks. Many stone-coated products are tested to UL 2218 Class 4, the highest impact rating, which involves dropping a 2-inch steel ball from 20 feet without cracking the product. DECRA's published warranty covers hail up to 2.5 inches in diameter.

Wind resistance

Interlocking, mechanically fastened panels resist uplift well. DECRA's warranty covers wind up to 120 mph. For context, the residential design wind speed in St. Charles County's building code is 107 mph, and St. Peters uses 115 mph.

Light weight

According to Fixr's national cost guide, stone-coated steel weighs under 2 pounds per square foot, compared with roughly 8 to 12 pounds for tile. You get a tile or shake appearance without the structural load that real tile can demand.

Long manufacturer warranties

DECRA's published warranty is a lifetime limited, non-prorated warranty, and DECRA says it is transferable within 20 years. Non-prorated matters: coverage does not shrink each year the way a prorated warranty does. Other brands have different terms, so read the specific warranty for the product you choose.

Possible insurance benefits

Some insurers offer discounts for roofs rated UL 2218 Class 4. Whether yours does, and how much, is a question for your agent.

The cons

Stone-coated steel is not the right roof for every house, and anyone selling it should say so.

Higher upfront cost

It costs more than asphalt shingles, often considerably more. The payback comes from longevity and fewer hail replacements, which only pays off if you stay in the house long enough to collect it.

Fewer installers

Stone-coated steel installs differently from shingles, with its own battens or direct-to-deck methods, trim pieces and flashing details. Fewer crews do it regularly, which can mean longer scheduling and less competition on price. Ask any installer how many stone-coated roofs they have done and follow the manufacturer's installation instructions, because warranty coverage can depend on them.

Dents are possible in extreme hail

Class 4 and a 2.5-inch warranty are not a promise against all hail. According to the National Weather Service, the March 14, 2024 storms produced 3 to 3.75-inch hail over St. Charles County, larger than DECRA's warranty threshold. Hail that size can dent steel. Our St. Charles County hail history lays out the recent storms so you can judge the risk for yourself.

Granule wear over the decades

Like asphalt shingles, the stone surface can shed some granules over the decades. It is generally gradual, but it is worth knowing about before you buy.

Repairs need matching parts

Replacing a damaged panel means sourcing the same profile and color from the same manufacturer. That is usually manageable for current product lines, harder for discontinued ones.

The noise question

This one is mostly a myth. A metal roof is loud over open framing on a barn. Stone-coated steel on a house goes over solid decking and underlayment, and the stone surface breaks up the sound of rain.

What it costs, and what drives the number

Fixr's 2021 national guide put stone-coated steel at about $7 to $15 per square foot installed. That range is national and several years old, so use it as a ballpark, not a quote. The things that move the price on a real house:

  • Roof size and pitch. Steeper and larger roofs take more material, staging and labor.
  • Profile. Shingle, shake and tile profiles differ in material and trim cost.
  • Complexity. Valleys, dormers, chimneys, skylights and wall intersections each need flashing and trim.
  • Tear-off and decking. Removing old layers and replacing rotted decking add cost. Under the IRC, a roof with two existing layers cannot get a third, so it needs a full tear-off.
  • Code details. St. Charles County and the City of St. Charles both rate the area's weathering as "Severe," and good practice includes ice and water shield in valleys and around penetrations even though the local codes don't require ice barrier.

Our guide to how much a new roof costs in St. Charles covers the broader numbers for shingle and metal roofs.

The St. Charles angle

Hail sizes here

The National Weather Service recorded 3 to 3.75-inch hail over St. Charles County on March 14, 2024, and 2.75-inch hail near Saint Charles on May 26, 2024. On April 28, 2012, golf-ball to baseball hail fell across the county. A roof that shrugs off 2-inch hail is a real upgrade here, but the biggest storms can exceed any product's rating.

Historic districts

Inside the City of St. Charles, the six local historic districts are a different story. Changing a roof's material or appearance generally needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Landmarks Board. Frenchtown's design guidelines list asphalt, fiberglass, wood shingles, slate and synthetic slate as replacement materials, and metal is not on that list. If you own a home in Frenchtown or near Historic Main Street, call the city's preservation planner before you settle on stone-coated steel. Our historic home roofing page explains how we plan material choices around that review.

Where it fits best

Stone-coated steel suits larger, newer homes outside the historic districts, such as the brick-front homes and golf-course lots of Weldon Spring, and homeowners in St. Peters or Cottleville who are tired of replacing shingles after hail. Check your subdivision's indentures or HOA for roofing material and color rules first.

Stone-coated steel is a strong hail roof for St. Charles County, but no roof is hail-proof, and none is right for every house.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does stone-coated steel roofing cost?

Fixr's national cost guide (2021) put stone-coated steel at roughly $7 to $15 per square foot installed. That is a national figure, not a St. Charles price, and it is several years old. Your actual cost depends on roof size, pitch, profile, tear-off and decking condition. We give a free written quote after looking at the roof, so you see a real number for your house.

Is stone-coated steel hail-proof?

No roof is hail-proof. Stone-coated steel holds up very well in hail, and DECRA's published warranty covers hail up to 2.5 inches. But the National Weather Service documented 3 to 3.75-inch hail over St. Charles County on March 14, 2024, which is larger than that. Extreme hail can dent steel panels, so treat the material as highly resistant, not immune.

Is a stone-coated steel roof loud in the rain?

Generally not. The noise worry comes from metal panels fastened over open framing, like a barn. Stone-coated steel on a house is installed over solid roof decking with underlayment, and the stone-chip surface helps break up the sound of rain and hail. Most homeowners notice little difference from a shingle roof once they are inside.

What warranty comes with stone-coated steel?

It depends on the manufacturer. DECRA's published warranty is a lifetime limited, non-prorated warranty that covers wind up to 120 mph and hail up to 2.5 inches, and DECRA says it is transferable within 20 years. Always read the current terms on the manufacturer's site for the exact product you are buying, since registration and installation requirements apply.

Can I put stone-coated steel on a house in a St. Charles historic district?

Possibly, but expect review. In the city's local historic districts, changing a roof's material or appearance generally requires a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Landmarks Board. Frenchtown's design guidelines list asphalt, fiberglass, wood shingles, slate and synthetic slate as replacement materials and do not list metal. Call the city's preservation planner before choosing a material.

How much does stone-coated steel weigh compared with tile?

Much less. Fixr's national guide puts stone-coated steel at under 2 pounds per square foot, compared with roughly 8 to 12 pounds per square foot for tile. That light weight is one reason homeowners choose it for a tile or shake look without the structural load that real tile can require.
